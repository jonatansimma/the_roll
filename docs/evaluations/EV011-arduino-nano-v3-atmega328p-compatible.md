## Objective and operating principle

Assess whether a **classic ATmega328P-based, Nano V3.0-form-factor development board** offers a simpler and affordable controller alternative for the Roll's **off-dog sensor-to-LED experiments**, especially a light-touch contact sensor producing a digital event. Firmware would read the external sensor, implement timing/filtering and drive an appropriately current-limited LED stage. The board has **no integrated bite, touch, accelerometer or gyroscope sensor** and therefore cannot detect deliberate retrieval by itself. Identical-looking variants may differ in electrical implementation.

## Expected advantages, limitations, and uncertainties

- **Advantages:** mature Arduino IDE toolchain and very simple software for switch/capacitive touch input, LED output, debouncing and configurable indication duration; no unwanted radio functions. Pin rows and ISP header simplify inexpensive off-dog debugging. AVR interrupts can support external sensing wake-up when configured appropriately.
- **Basic-sensor potential:** Suitable in principle for a digital-output touch sensor or mechanical switch, with pull-up/input wiring correctly chosen. It can also read accelerometer data by I²C, but is less capable than RP2040 or ESP32-C3 boards for larger simultaneous sensor logs, filtering algorithms and advanced classification.
- **Small-memory limitation:** **2 KB SRAM** leaves little room to buffer many samples of 3- or 6-axis data plus other state; prefer streaming/bounded buffers for experiments. A motion signature is not proven useful for intentional mouth retrieval.
- **Power disadvantages:** the reference board is **5 V at 16 MHz**. Whole-board idle/standby current may be dominated by USB–serial converter, regulator, power LED and firmware. Low **ATmega328P-chip** sleep current **cannot** establish a low-power Nano V3 board or the preferred Roll runtime.
- **Clone uncertainty:** input-voltage rail, 3.3 V pin current capability, USB/back-feed circuitry, oscillator, bootloader, USB drivers and solder quality are not confirmed for the photographed board. The 3.3 V output of a classic Nano, where present, is **not** an unrestricted sensor power rail.
- **No dedicated charger/battery protection or embedded motion sensor:** add power conditioning and sensor modules as necessary. A complete build needs vibration, impact, moisture and secure component-retention testing.

## Integration implications

### Requirement-fit review (not compliance)

- **REQ005 physical integration:** The reference **45 × 18 mm** board dimensions are **plausibly** compatible with an axial layout within the [nominal cylindrical external envelope](../requirements.md#dimensional-and-mass-targets). **Width less than outside diameter does not establish actual internal fit**, particularly with enclosure walls, USB socket, mounting/headers, sensor, LEDs, protected battery, wires and attachment loop. Actual board mass is unmeasured; reference **~7 g** cannot verify the overall **50 g total target**.
- **REQ004 battery operation:** Stable **5 V** supply is required for an unmodified **16 MHz** Nano reference board. The official Nano documents Mini-B USB 5 V, a regulated 5 V supply pin and a higher external **VIN** supply for onboard regulation. **The pictured clone's VIN/regulator limits are unverified.** A single nominal 3.7 V Li-Po or **three AA/AAA cells** must **not** be assumed to supply a stable 5 V logic rail over their discharge range; nominal 4.5 V from three alkaline cells is already below the reference 5 V system voltage. A suitable regulated boost/supply architecture, protection and power isolation would be required if such batteries are used. **Do not apply a raw 3.7 V Li-Po to the 5V or 3V3 rail**, and do not assume simultaneous USB and externally powered operation is safe without analyzing the particular board's power path.
- **REQ001–REQ003 sensing/LED:** No on-board retrieval detector. Digital sensor inputs are straightforward if voltage compatible. Most external 3.3 V-only IMU modules (e.g. [EV009 LIS3DH](EV009-electrokit-ekm029-lis3dh.md)) **cannot be connected to a 5 V I²C bus or 5 V output without checking pull-ups and level shifting**. The 3.3 V output on an unverified clone is **not** a substitute for validated level compatibility. Drive external handler-visible LEDs with appropriately rated resistors/transistor rather than treating a GPIO as a general LED power supply.
- **REQ002 and behavioral context:** Light grip, brief drops and re-pickups (reported and recorded only in the **proposed** [agent_main requirements update](../requirements.md#reported-retrieval-behavior--design-consideration-not-a-requirement)) require testable event handling, but do not create an ATmega-specific detection advantage. Thresholds and trigger/reset policy remain unresolved.
- **REQ006–REQ009 environment/safety:** Exposed board, loose headers and USB connector are only suitable for off-dog work until fully enclosed. Assess electrical/thermal faults, battery retention, impact/fragments, saliva and snagging/attachment under the [mandatory safety gate](../safety.md). This board is not cleared for dog contact.

## Proposed evaluation experiments

**Proposed only; none performed.** Follow the [test recording rules](../../tests/README.md), document exact board, firmware commit, power source, wiring and defined conditions.

1. **Identify physical board**: Obtain both sides (including readable MCU marking and USB–serial chip), confirm pad labeling, oscillator/regulator identity, mounting and installed header configuration; measure mass, length/width/height and check solder quality/damage.
2. **USB bring-up and bootloader**: With a known-good USB supply and appropriate cable, verify enumeration and upload a minimal Arduino Nano/ATmega328P program. If uploads fail, check **old vs new bootloader** selection and interface/driver; record actual MCU/fuse/bootloader where possible.
3. **Sensor + LED functionality**: With a simple switch or properly level-matched 3.3 V/5 V-compatible touch module, timestamp simulated pickup/release/re-pickup, false activation from routine handling and LED response; verify output-stage and regulator ratings.
4. **I²C compatibility**: If using a 3.3 V-only sensor (e.g. LIS3DH), validate **sensor supply, SDA/SCL pull-ups and bidirectional level shifting**. Scan bus address before logging data and verify pin definitions (A4 SDA, A5 SCL on the classic Nano).
5. **Full-board power comparison**: Measure USB or 5 V regulated input current during idle sensing, interrupts/sleep and LED events, including the USB interface/LED/regulator overhead. Measure active vs sleep-and-wake latency and whether the chosen touch sensor can wake the AVR from a supported sleep mode.
6. **Battery/run-time and pack check**: Test a *verified*, protected power solution through full usable discharge while logging rail voltage and current; compare with both [runtime thresholds](../requirements.md#operating-time-requirements). Never assume a chip-only power number is a tested Nano consumption value.
7. **Integrated 3D and hazard check**: Make a component mass budget and interior mockup, check mounting, bite/impact containment, temperature and water exposure **off-dog**, and progress only via the [documented safety gate](../safety.md).

## Conclusion and next action

- **Assessment:** **Promising for straightforward off-dog digital sensing and LED control, but lower priority for Project ROLL's current development than the existing Pico WH approach. Requires evidence before hardware selection.**
- **Reasoning:** Simplicity, maturity and inexpensive Nano-compatible hardware make a useful fallback if testing shows that a light-touch digital sensor suffices. However, the unverified clone identity, **5 V/16 MHz** supply burden, 3.3 V sensor-level translation, USB/regulator power overhead and very limited data buffers reduce its advantages for a lightweight, battery-powered, possibly motion-assisted system. No controller has demonstrated a complete compliant Roll configuration.
- **Next action / decision link:** If obtained inexpensively, inspect the PCB back and chip marking, upload a minimal sketch, and compare **full-board current and sensor-to-LED latency** with [P001 Pico WH](../../prototypes/P001-pico-wh/README.md) using the same supply profiles and test conditions. Record outcomes in [tests](../../tests/README.md), then make any hardware selection in [decisions](../decisions.md). Do not replace the planned controller or modify a prototype merely because this evaluation is created.
